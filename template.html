    How to read this

    • Wave height is the size of the wave face when it breaks, in feet. "Knee to waist high" means it would reach about that high on an adult standing in the water.
    • Wind matters most. Wind blowing from land out to sea (offshore) makes waves smooth and clean. Wind blowing from the sea onto the beach (onshore) makes them choppy.
    • Period is the seconds between waves. Bigger numbers (10+ seconds) mean waves traveled from far away and have more power and better shape. Florida usually sees 6–9 seconds.
    • Tide is the water level. Most Florida beaches break best around the middle of the tide, especially while it is rising.
    • The score (0–10) adds up size, period, wind, and tide. The "Why this score" bars in each spot show exactly where the points came from.
    • Safety comes first. The flag colors follow Florida beach flags. Rip currents are the number one danger at the beach. If you get caught in one, don't fight it — swim parallel to shore, then back in.
    Forecasts are estimates. Always check conditions and lifeguard flags at the beach before going in. Not an official NWS product.
